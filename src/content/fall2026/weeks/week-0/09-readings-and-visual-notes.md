Read two essays this weekend. Make handwritten visual notes on each one. The SQ5R method has seven steps: survey, question, read, recite, record, review, reflect.

- Cam Pedersen's [“Code is Clay”](https://campedersen.com/code-is-clay).
- Chia Amisola's [“Making space for a handmade web”](https://www.figma.com/blog/making-space-for-a-handmade-web/).

Treat the essays as practitioner perspectives, not proof.

## The seven steps, for each essay

**Survey.** Do this before you read.

- Look at the title, the author, the headings, the images, and the first and last paragraphs.
- Write the title and the author at the top of a page.
- Write one sentence on what you expect the essay to argue.

**Question.**

- Copy one leading question from the list below.
- Write one question of your own.

**Read.**

- Read the whole essay once.

**Recite.**

- Close the essay.
- Write two sentences, in your own words, on what it argued.
- Check your two sentences against the essay.

**Record.**

- Make your visual notes: words, sketches, diagrams, arrows.

**Review.** Do this the next day.

- Reread your notes, not the essay.
- Answer your two questions on the page.
- Star the one thing you would bring to a discussion.

**Reflect.**

- Write one or two sentences on which of your four introduction answers this essay challenges or changes.

## Leading questions

**“Code is Clay”**

- Pedersen made a hypercube in a ceramics class, not a mug. What does that say about what making is for?
- He writes, "Delete it. Rewrite it. Start fresh." What work have you thrown away? What did you keep from it?
- He writes that the craft "got more valuable once it wasn't necessary anymore." What would that look like for code?

**“Making space for a handmade web”**

- Amisola describes early webpages as handmade, amateur and unpolished. What in your own feeds is the opposite?
- She imagines a website that isn't always awake, one that's hard to find, and one without ownership. Which would you build? Who would it be for?
- If your site did not answer to a million people, what would it answer to?

**Across both essays**

- Which idea in these essays challenges or changes your answer to "What do you think a coder is?"
