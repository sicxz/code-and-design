# Explain your CSS

**Weekly assignments · 10 points · Due 2026-10-11 at 11:59 p.m. Pacific**

## Studio brief

Connect a CSS declaration to an observed visual effect. This is the evidence from [week 2](/1/weeks/week-2/), not an additional project. Finish the same experiment begun in studio.

## Build sequence

1. Select one rule controlling type, width, margin, or padding.
2. Write a prediction, change one value, and refresh.
3. Compare the result with your prediction; if different, inspect the selector and active rule.
4. Save the version that demonstrates the relationship. If recovering after the October 7 check, use the assigned smaller example.

## Source check

Name the selector, property, original/new values, and actual effect. Explain margin versus padding if that was your experiment.

## Studio evidence

Submit in Canvas:

- **One artifact:** Your recipe or the source-recovery artifact.
- **A brief explanation:** two to four sentences addressing the source check above.
- **One question or next step:** something specific enough for a person to respond to.

## Delivery check and rubric

Open the submitted link while signed out. If publishing or an online service is blocked, attach your current HTML/CSS or planning export with a one-sentence access note. Do not submit a private editor link that only you can open.

The [weekly rubric](/1/resources/rubrics/) awards 4 points for the requested experiment, 4 for its source explanation, and 2 for the question/next step and usable delivery. It does not grade the surrounding project's finish. No extra reflection page, repeated transcript, or decoration is required.

**AI allowance:** The experiment and explanation are student AI-free. This brief documents your own source reasoning even if submitted after October 7.
