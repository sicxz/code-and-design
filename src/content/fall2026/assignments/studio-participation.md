# Studio participation

**Participation and engagement · 100 points across the term · Record closes December 9, 2026 at 3 p.m. Pacific**

## Studio brief

Learning in a studio means attempting the work, sharing observations, listening, and using feedback. Participation can be shown through discussion, pair work, written comments, specific questions, and other agreed accessible formats. Attendance helps make that exchange possible, but does not by itself establish engagement.

## What to do each week

Bring the current artifact or files, attempt the guided exercise, and use the project block to make progress. During a critique, describe something observable and connect it to the reader's experience: “At the narrow width, this line leaves the screen” is useful feedback. Listen to the response and try a repair when feedback reveals a problem.

If you will miss or interrupt a session, communicate through Canvas Inbox or instructor email and ask how to demonstrate the missed practice. Do not publish personal circumstances in the course repository. The October 28 asynchronous attempt and the November 2 follow-up can show the same preparation, engagement, and communication without a live instructor meeting on the absence date.

## Evidence and assessment

The instructor records evidence across the term; students do not need an additional attendance essay, screenshot log, or final upload. If a contribution was not visible to the instructor, bring the relevant artifact or a concise explanation during a check-in.

The [participation rubric](/1/resources/rubrics/) awards prepared studio practice 40 points, peer exchange 30, and follow-through/communication 30. Scores reflect the pattern across available opportunities using the shared performance levels. This is the single record in the 20% participation category; weekly submission quality and finished projects have their own categories.

This revised brief creates no automatic absence-count failure or late-point rule. The instructor discusses individual arrangements through the course's current Canvas communication and university processes.
