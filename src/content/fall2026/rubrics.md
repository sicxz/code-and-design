# Assessment rubrics

The course uses four weighted categories: participation 20%, weekly assignments 30%, projects 40%, and final portfolio 10%. Assess the learning named in the brief; extra decoration, extra pages, paid tools, and long explanations do not earn automatic extra credit.

## Weekly assignments

Weekly assignments have different point values. Some are graded complete or incomplete. Each assignment in Canvas shows its points.

## Projects · 100 points each

Each project has its own criteria and point allocations in its brief. Use these performance levels within each criterion:

- **Demonstrated:** full points when the named behavior works and the student explains it.
- **Mostly demonstrated:** 75% of criterion points when the core works with a limited omission or inconsistency.
- **Developing:** 50% when part is visible but a substantial gap affects use or understanding.
- **Attempted:** 25% when there is a relevant attempt with little working evidence.
- **Not evidenced:** 0% when no relevant work is available.

A technical fault should be scored in its relevant criterion rather than deducted repeatedly from unrelated criteria. A source check contributes to the project's source-understanding criterion. Checkpoints and design planning are part of that project, not extra project assignments.

## Final portfolio · 100 points

| Criterion | Points | Full-credit evidence |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Curation and introductions | 30 | Four project entries clearly identify the work and its purpose, with a concise personal introduction. |
| Navigation and delivery | 25 | Each project and source link opens, return paths work, and the published site matches the submitted version. |
| Readability and access | 20 | The portfolio itself works at narrow and wide widths, supports keyboard navigation, and communicates through readable text. |
| Source presentation and growth | 25 | A brief live or agreed equivalent explanation traces one design decision to code and identifies a concrete improvement in the student's understanding. |

Use the same performance levels as projects. Do not re-grade the four projects inside this category. They are evidence to curate, not four new builds. Repairs that improve project navigation are welcome without adding a new assignment.

## Participation and engagement

Participation and engagement is 20% of your course grade. It includes one in-class item every class day, worth 5 points, and other participation and engagement assignments that are not daily. The [syllabus](/1/syllabus/#attendance) explains the daily item.

## Keep the categories distinct

A weekly assignment may point to a work-in-progress project to show one experiment. The weekly score concerns that experiment and explanation; the project score concerns the finished deliverable. The portfolio score concerns its collection, navigation, and presentation. Avoid making students repeat the same evidence in separate reflection documents.
